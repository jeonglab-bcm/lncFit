Findings

  • Sequence vs expression is real for the sequence side. Trained beats untrained in HAP1, MDA-MB-231 and THP1 by 7–11 z, though seeds vary a lot.
  • Correction to step 2: the expression embedding's gain was mostly the network itself. Untrained, it already scores nearly as high (THP1 22.3 vs 27.1).
  • Contrastive learning against tissue specificity does not help. Its sequence embedding barely beats untrained, and its tissue side only matches the raw 8-number profile. Sequence says little about which organs a lncRNA is expressed in.
  • K562 is high even for untrained sequence encoders: the GC bias again.

Findings

  • Tissue patterns are a continuum, not clusters. Silhouette is about 0.3 for every k from 4 to 12.
  • Testis-only lncRNAs are depleted of essential ones in MDA-MB-231 and THP1 (0.61×, 0.69×), and lower in HAP1 and K562. They are the largest group (9,494).
  • Broader lncRNAs are enriched: the testis + ovary group (tau 0.75) reaches 1.47× in THP1. With a stricter 1 RPKM cut-off, the broad, well-expressed cluster was 2.2× enriched in MDA-MB-231 and THP1 (p < 0.001).
  • Same direction as before: essential lncRNAs are broadly and more highly expressed. HEK293FT again shows nothing.

SSL 2: first contrastive run, sequence vs expression 2026-10-03

Two MLP encoders trained so each lncRNA's sequence embedding (3/4/5-mers) matches its own expression embedding (developmental atlas + cell-line TPM). Symmetric InfoNCE, 60 epochs, on 31,661 lncRNAs (26,165 never screened). No essentiality labels used in training. Then the EDA-1 neighbour test on the 5,496 screened lncRNAs. One seed. Script: contrastive/train_clip.py, numbers: contrastive/clip_v1_eval.json.

GC R²: how much of a lncRNA's GC content a linear model reads back from the embedding (5-fold CV).

Findings

  • Contrastive training helps both views in HAP1, MDA-MB-231 and THP1, without seeing a label. The sequence embedding gains most: HAP1 z 2.9 → 12.3, MDA-MB-231 8.9 → 16.0. Pairing with expression teaches the sequence encoder which features matter.
  • The two views are only weakly linked. Held-out retrieval reaches top-1% accuracy 0.09 (chance 0.01) and stops improving after about epoch 30 while training loss keeps falling.
  • GC leaks into the expression embedding (R² 0.31 raw → 0.49 learned). Its K562 jump (6.1 → 16.8) may be the GC bias carried over from the sequence side, so K562 gains don't count yet.
  • HEK293FT stays flat.

Before trusting this: (1) repeat over seeds; (2) compare with the same encoders untrained or trained without the cross-view pairing, to show the contrastive pairing is what helps; (3) remove GC from the sequence view.

Findings

  • The best embedding depends on the cell line. The developmental atlas is best for HAP1, MDA-MB-231 and THP1; sequence embeddings are best for K562.
  • K562's sequence signal is mostly a GC bias, likely technical. GC content alone gives AUROC 0.75 for K562 essentiality and 0.47–0.53 in every other line. K562 hits pile up on GC-rich chromosomes (chr19: 14% of lncRNAs essential in K562 vs 1–3% in other lines). Not genomic proximity: excluding neighbours within 1 Mb changes nothing.
  • HEK293FT shows no structure in any embedding (all z ≤ 1.2). Its labels may be mostly noise.
  • Open lead: k-mers keep a modest signal in HAP1, MDA-MB-231 and THP1 (z 7–9) that GC + length do not explain (z ≈ 0).
  • Effect sizes are modest: the best per-line AUPRC is 1.5–2.6× the positive rate.

Next: remove GC from the sequence embeddings and re-test K562 and the other lines; then look at the developmental atlas per organ and stage.
