EDA 6: what are the lncRNAs essential in three or more lines? 2026-10-04

EDA 5 showed that the 40 screened lncRNAs essential in three or more of the five cell lines (the core set) are the most separable group, including by sequence embeddings that GC and length do not explain. We therefore described them against the 4,484 lncRNAs never essential, using only pre-screen annotation (mmc2 S1A, expression), and tested explanations for the sequence signal: that it reflects multi-copy families, or neighbouring housekeeping genes. Fisher exact tests for categories, Mann-Whitney U for numbers. Script: eda/core_shared.py, annotations: eda/core_shared_annotations.csv.

Findings

  • The core lncRNAs are broadly and highly expressed. Their median tau is 0.34 against 1.00, and they are expressed in all five cell lines. This is the property that the expression embeddings pick up (developmental atlas z 54).
  • The sequence signal is concentrated in a few lncRNAs. The ten most connected core lncRNAs hold 78% of all core-to-core links among k-mer neighbours, and 18 of the 40 have at least one core neighbour (4.6% of neighbours are core, against 0.9% by chance). Removing the nine multimember-family members cuts the k-mer z from 39.6 to 7.9, and removing three whose neighbour is DepMap-essential cuts it to 22.2, whereas the atlas z stays above 35 in both cases. The large z for k-mers therefore rests on a small group, and should not be read as a general sequence effect.
  • Neighbouring housekeeping genes do not explain the set. The closest protein-coding genes are only slightly more essential in DepMap (median −0.09 vs −0.01), and the distance to them does not differ, although individual cases (TAF12, −0.78) are consistent with a shared mechanism.
  • Multi-copy families are over-represented (9 of 40, 22% vs 5.6%). A guide against one family member could knock down several copies, but a search of the whole library against all 31,678 annotated transcripts (PR #130) found almost no shared targets, so any such copies would have to be missing from the annotation.

We acknowledge that 40 lncRNAs is small, that the comparisons above were not corrected for multiple testing, and that the family explanation is a hypothesis: we have not located the unannotated copies. Further work should check the nine family members for unannotated genomic copies of their guide targets.

EDA 5: cell-line-specific versus shared essential lncRNAs 2026-10-04

We next asked whether the signal seen so far comes from lncRNAs that are essential in one cell line or from those essential in several. We therefore split the 5,496 screened lncRNAs into never essential (4,484), essential in one line only (HAP1 146, HEK293FT 191, K562 304, MDA-MB-231 80, THP1 117) and shared (essential in two or more lines, 174; three or more, 40). For each group we tested whether an embedding places its members close together among {group} and {never essential} (same 20-neighbour test as EDA 1, z against 100 shuffles). Script: eda/line_specific_separation.py, numbers: eda/line_specific_separation.csv.

Findings

  • Most of the signal found so far belongs to lncRNAs essential in several cell lines. The 40 lncRNAs essential in three or more lines are by far the most separable group (developmental atlas z 50.2, k-mer 35.9), and GC + length does not explain it (0.3).
  • Cell-line-specific essential lncRNAs mostly do not cluster in any embedding. HAP1, HEK293FT and MDA-MB-231 show nothing (z at most 2.4), and THP1 shows only a weak expression signal (4.9).
  • K562 is the exception, mainly through sequence (z 20.7–34.5), but GC + length already reaches 12.6, which fits the partial GC explanation from EDA 4.
  • Overlap between lines is only modestly above chance. If the five lines were independent we would expect 97 / 4 / 0 / 0 lncRNAs essential in exactly 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 lines; we observed 134 / 24 / 9 / 7. About three quarters of the two-line overlaps may therefore be chance, whereas the three-or-more-line group is a real shared set.
  • Among lncRNAs essential somewhere, single-line and shared ones are only weakly separable (cell-line expression z 5.0, atlas 3.0, sequence 0.9–2.8).

We acknowledge that single-line labels combine genuine line-specific biology with chance hits at the p < 0.05 threshold, which cannot be separated with these data. The sizes of the line-specific groups (80–304) also limit what can be detected. Further work should look at which lncRNAs form the three-or-more-line group, and why they separate by sequence.

EDA 4: separation inside tissue-specificity clusters 2026-10-04

We hypothesized that essential lncRNAs group together only among lncRNAs that share a tissue pattern, so that pooling every tissue group (EDA 1) would hide the signal. We therefore found each lncRNA's 20 nearest neighbours inside its tissue cluster from EDA 2, after standardising and PCA within that cluster, and scored the essential fraction among them against 100 label shuffles (z). In addition to the five cell-line labels we tested an essential in at least one cell line label, which pools the positives (1,012 of 5,496). Tests with fewer than 15 positives were skipped. Findings

  • The hypothesis holds in some tissue clusters and not in others. Among testis + ovary lncRNAs, expression embeddings separate essential lncRNAs strongly (THP1 z 17.1, MDA-MB-231 9.6), and the same is true in the liver cluster. Among testis-only and cerebellum + brain lncRNAs, expression embeddings show nothing (z between −0.6 and 2.1).
  • The useful embedding depends on the cluster. Expression separates in testis + ovary and liver; sequence embeddings separate in liver (HAP1: DNABERT-2 9.2, k-mer 6.1) and, for K562, in every cluster.
  • Pooling the cell lines (“essential in at least one”) gives a weaker but broader signal. Overall, sequence embeddings reach z 6.9–8.5. Inside clusters they stay positive (0.8–5.2 across four clusters) while GC + length does not (−0.2 to 0.8), so this part is not a GC effect.
  • Correction to EDA 1. We described K562's sequence signal as mostly a GC bias. Inside tissue clusters, sequence embeddings still reach z 5.5–15.1 for K562, whereas GC + length reaches 2.0–5.6. GC explains part of it, not all; chromosome or repeat content remain candidates.

We acknowledge several limitations. Positives per line and cluster range from 19 to 117, the clusters were defined from the atlas (so the atlas row is not independent of the grouping), and about 280 tests were run with one set of shuffles, so isolated z values below 3.5 should not be read as findings. Further work should repeat the test with chromosome and repeat content as controls.

Findings

  • The best embedding depends on the cell line. The developmental atlas is best for HAP1, MDA-MB-231 and THP1; sequence embeddings are best for K562.
  • K562's sequence signal is mostly a GC bias, likely technical. GC content alone gives AUROC 0.75 for K562 essentiality and 0.47–0.53 in every other line. K562 hits pile up on GC-rich chromosomes (chr19: 14% of lncRNAs essential in K562 vs 1–3% in other lines). Not genomic proximity: excluding neighbours within 1 Mb changes nothing.
  • HEK293FT shows no structure in any embedding (all z ≤ 1.2). Its labels may be mostly noise.
  • Open lead: k-mers keep a modest signal in HAP1, MDA-MB-231 and THP1 (z 7–9) that GC + length do not explain (z ≈ 0).
  • Effect sizes are modest: the best per-line AUPRC is 1.5–2.6× the positive rate.

Next: remove GC from the sequence embeddings and re-test K562 and the other lines; then look at the developmental atlas per organ and stage.
